      How this works & key rules
      • Distance. A journey qualifies at roughly 81 km (48 Hashimi miles) for the Maliki, Shafiʿi and Hanbali schools, and about 77 km in the common Hanafi estimate. Scholars give estimates from about 77 to 89 km. This app uses straight-line distance, which is never longer than the real route. So a leg marked borderline may still qualify by road.
      • When it starts and ends. Concessions begin once you leave your town's built-up area and end when you arrive back home.
      • Becoming a resident. If you intend to stay in one place long enough, you pray in full there. That means 15 days or more (Hanafi), 4 full days or more not counting the days you arrive and leave (Maliki, Shafiʿi), or more than 4 days (Hanbali). When you leave that place, a new journey begins and has to meet the distance again.
      • Several stops. While you are still a traveller, short hops between stops stay part of the same journey, even if a single hop is short.
      • Qasr shortens only the 4-rakʿah prayers (Zuhr, Asr, Isha) to 2. Fajr and Maghrib never change. If you pray behind a resident imam, pray in full with him.
      • Jamʿ (combining) joins Zuhr with Asr, or Maghrib with Isha. Hanafis don't allow it, except at ʿArafah and Muzdalifah during Hajj. Malikis allow it while you are actually on the road. Shafiʿis and Hanbalis allow it for as long as you are a traveller.
      • This is general guidance based on the main positions of each school. For your own situation, please ask a qualified scholar.
